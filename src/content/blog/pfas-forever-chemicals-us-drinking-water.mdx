In April 2024, the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) finalized the first-ever federal drinking water standard for PFAS — per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — setting legally enforceable limits for six specific PFAS compounds at levels as low as 4 parts per trillion (ppt). The response from public health experts was a mixture of relief and alarm: the new standard acknowledged the danger officially, but the scale of the contamination it was trying to address is staggering.

The EPA estimates that **over 200 million Americans** — more than 60% of the US population — are exposed to PFAS in their drinking water above the levels now recognized as concerning for human health. The Environmental Working Group (EWG) has documented PFAS contamination in water supplies in all 50 states.

More than 70% of US adults are concerned about exposure to harmful chemicals in their food and water, according to a 2023 Pew Research Center survey. That concern is well-founded.

> **Expert Insight:**
> *"PFAS are unlike virtually any other environmental toxin we deal with in medicine because they are genuinely persistent — they do not break down in the environment or in the human body,"* explains preventative cardiologist Dr. Michael Chen. *"From a cardiovascular standpoint, the research linking PFAS exposure to dyslipidemia, hypertension, and cardiac function changes is concerning. Combined with the thyroid disruption, immune suppression, and carcinogenic effects documented in the literature, PFAS represent one of the most significant environmental health challenges facing American patients today. And the disturbing reality is that most people have no idea they're being exposed."*

## What Are PFAS? Understanding "Forever Chemicals"

PFAS (pronounced "P-FAS") stands for per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — a family of over **12,000 synthetic chemical compounds** that have been manufactured and used in industrial and consumer products since the 1940s. Their defining characteristic is the carbon-fluorine bond, one of the strongest in all of chemistry, which makes them:

- **Extraordinarily resistant to heat** (used in non-stick cookware, fireproof clothing)
- **Resistant to water and grease** (used in food packaging, stain-resistant carpets, waterproof clothing)
- **Non-biodegradable in virtually all environmental conditions** — hence the name "forever chemicals"

The US military began using PFAS-containing aqueous film-forming foam (AFFF) at military airfields and naval installations in the 1970s. When AFFF is used in training exercises and firefighting drills, PFAS leach into the soil and groundwater, contaminating wells and municipal water systems for miles in every direction. Over 700 US military installations have confirmed PFAS groundwater contamination.

Beyond military sources, PFAS enter water supplies through:
- Industrial discharge from PFAS manufacturers (3M, DuPont, and their spinoffs)
- Landfill leachate from disposed PFAS-containing products
- Agricultural application of PFAS-contaminated biosolids (sewage sludge used as fertilizer — an alarmingly common practice that EPA's own data suggests has contaminated farmland in all 50 states)
- Food processing facilities using PFAS-containing equipment

## What PFAS Do to the Human Body

PFAS accumulate in human blood, organs, and tissues over time — a property called **bioaccumulation**. The average American has measurable PFAS in their blood, a finding confirmed in the CDC's National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), which has tracked PFAS blood levels in the US population since 1999.

Clinical and epidemiological research has linked elevated PFAS blood levels to:

### Cancer
- **Kidney cancer:** Multiple studies show statistically significant increased risk. A 2020 study in *Environmental Health Perspectives* found PFOA exposure associated with significantly elevated renal cell carcinoma risk.
- **Testicular cancer:** A 2020 study analyzing Danish and US military populations found elevated PFAS levels strongly correlated with testicular cancer risk.
- **Breast cancer:** Emerging research suggests PFAS may mimic estrogen-signaling, potentially promoting hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer.

### Thyroid Disease
PFAS interfere with thyroid hormone synthesis and transport. A comprehensive 2017 analysis in *Environment International* found significant associations between PFAS exposure and both hypothyroidism and thyroid hormone disruption, even at low-level exposure.

### Immune Suppression
One of the most alarming findings: PFAS exposure — even at the levels found commonly in children's blood — suppresses antibody responses to vaccines. A 2020 *JAMA Pediatrics* study found that children with higher PFAS blood levels had weaker immune responses to standard childhood vaccinations, with some children failing to maintain protective antibody levels.

### Metabolic and Cardiovascular Effects
- **Dyslipidemia:** PFAS exposure is consistently associated with higher total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, even at exposures below the new EPA limits
- **Hypertension:** Studies show PFAS exposure increases blood pressure through direct renal effects
- **Obesity:** PFAS interfere with metabolic setpoint regulation, potentially contributing to the difficulty people experience losing weight despite dietary changes

### Reproductive Health
- **Reduced fertility in both sexes**
- **Pregnancy complications:** PFAS exposure is associated with preeclampsia, gestational hypertension, and lower birth weight
- **Endocrine disruption:** PFAS mimic sex hormones, potentially disrupting puberty timing in children

## How to Find Out If Your Water Is Contaminated

**Step 1: Check the EWG Tap Water Database**
The Environmental Working Group maintains the most comprehensive PFAS water contaminant database for US municipal water supplies: [https://www.ewg.org/tapwater/](https://www.ewg.org/tapwater/). Enter your zip code to see what has been detected in your municipal supply.

**Step 2: Review Your Annual Water Quality Report (CCR)**
All US public water utilities are legally required to publish a Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) every July. Your water company must mail this to you or make it available online. Check for any PFAS listings.

**Step 3: Test Your Specific Tap**
If you have a private well, your water is NOT tested by any government agency. You must test it yourself. Several certified laboratories offer PFAS testing for well water for $200–400. The EPA recommends using a lab certified by your state's drinking water program.

## What Filters Actually Remove PFAS

This is where accurate information is critical. Most water filters on the market do NOT remove PFAS. Standard pitcher filters (Brita, etc.) using granular activated carbon have minimal effectiveness against PFAS at typical concentrations.

| Filter Type | PFAS Removal Rate | Cost Range | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reverse Osmosis (RO)** | 90–99% | $200–600 installed | Best option for under-sink; removes most contaminants |
| **Activated Carbon Block** (pitcher/undersink) | 50–80% | $30–150 | Variable; must be NSF 58/42 certified |
| **Ion Exchange Resin** | 90–99% | $300–800 | Highly effective; used in commercial systems |
| **Granular Activated Carbon (GAC)** | 20–60% | $100–400 | Standard pitcher filters; not sufficient alone |
| **Distillation** | ~99% | $150–300 | Effective but slow; energy-intensive |
| **Refrigerator Filters** | 0–30% | Included in appliance | Most do NOT remove PFAS |

**NSF Certification matters:** Look for filters certified under NSF/ANSI Standard 58 (reverse osmosis) or NSF/ANSI Standard 53 for reduced PFAS claims. This is the only validated standard for PFAS removal in consumer products.

## Reducing Your Exposure Beyond Water

**Non-stick cookware:** PTFE (Teflon) coatings are technically stable at normal cooking temperatures, but scratched or overheated non-stick pans can release PFAS compounds. Switch to cast iron, stainless steel, or ceramic cookware.

**Food packaging:** Microwave popcorn bags, fast food wrappers, and pizza boxes often contain PFAS coatings to resist grease. Minimize use or allow foods to cool before contact with packaging.

**Stain-resistant coatings:** Scotchgard-treated carpets, furniture, and clothing contain PFAS. Look for PFAS-free alternatives when replacing furnishings.

**Dental floss:** Traditional Teflon floss (Glide by Oral-B and some store brands) has been found to contain PFAS. Switch to PFAS-free alternatives (e.g., Cocofloss, Radius, natural silk flosses).

**Outdoor clothing:** Many "DWR" (durable water repellent) coatings on outdoor clothing use PFAS. Major brands including Patagonia and REI have committed to PFAS-free chemistry by 2025.

## The Policy Landscape: What Is Being Done

The EPA's April 2024 rule sets Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) for six PFAS:
- **PFOA and PFOS:** 4 ppt each (down from the 70 ppt advisory level)
- **PFNA, PFHxS, HFPO-DA (GenX):** 10 ppt each

Water utilities have 5 years (until 2029) to comply. Many smaller utilities in contaminated regions face significant costs to upgrade treatment technology, raising concerns about water rate increases in working-class communities.

**Ongoing litigation:** 3M settled PFAS contamination lawsuits for $10.3 billion in 2023. DuPont and Chemours settled for $1.18 billion. These settlements fund some — but not all — water system remediation costs.

## Practical Action Plan: Protecting Your Family Starting This Week

**Day 1:** Visit EWG.org/tapwater, enter your zip code, and review your municipal water report.

**Day 2:** If your water shows PFAS contamination, research NSF 58-certified reverse osmosis systems for under-sink installation.

**Day 3:** If you have a well, contact your state's water authority for a list of certified PFAS testing laboratories.

**Week 1:** Swap your non-stick pan for cast iron or stainless steel.

**Week 2:** Replace Teflon dental floss with a PFAS-free alternative.

**Ongoing:** Filter your water at the tap (for drinking and cooking), not at the whole-house level (impractical and expensive). Use the EWG database to track your local PFAS levels as new testing data becomes available.
